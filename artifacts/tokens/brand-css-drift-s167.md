**Sprint-167 m02. Point-in-time snapshot — not regenerated by any gate.**

`apps/explorer/src/styles/brand.css` is a hand-authored copy of the brand→semantic
mapping that predates the generated bridge. s167 m02 generates the same mapping from
the token sources. This file records, as data, every place the two disagree — so the
drift is a documented decision rather than something a build silently "fixed".

`brand.css` is NOT deleted this sprint: it has two importers
(`apps/explorer/src/stories/BrandA.stories.tsx:4`, `apps/explorer/src/styles/index.css:2`)
and removing it breaks `build-storybook`, a hard dependency of three CI jobs. It and
`layers.css` still WIN at runtime in the explorer app. Retirement is s168, and this
catalogue is the input to that decision.

## How this was derived

- **Hand side** — `brand.css`, with `var(--brandX-*)` indirections resolved to the
  primitive declared in that file's `[data-brand='X']` block. The base row is read
  from its `:where(...:not([data-theme]), ...[data-theme='light'])` rule.
- **Generated side** — the emitted `packages/tokens/dist/css/tokens.css` bridge block
  for the same cell.
- **Comparison set** — the 38 slots in `SEMANTIC_BRIDGE`
  (`packages/tokens/scripts/brand-bridge.mjs`) × 6 cells = 228 comparisons.

## Finding 1 — brand A base: the interactive-primary ramp is stale (3 slots)

The whole ramp drifted, not a single value. `brand.css` sits at hue **45** with the
lightness **0.02 below** each token. The token source carries a note that the newer
value was warmed and lifted deliberately to hold a contrast ratio
("Primary interactive background warmed +2° with +0.02 ΔL to maintain ≥4.5:1 contrast"),
so the hand-authored copy is the stale side and the generated side is correct.
The memo cited `brand.css:10` as one known instance; it is in fact all three.

| `--theme-surface-interactive-primary-default` | `oklch(0.56 0.19 45)` | `oklch(0.58 0.19 43)` |
| `--theme-surface-interactive-primary-hover` | `oklch(0.5 0.2 45)` | `oklch(0.52 0.2 43)` |
| `--theme-surface-interactive-primary-pressed` | `oklch(0.44 0.21 45)` | `oklch(0.46 0.21 43)` |

## Finding 2 — brand A dark: two entirely different designs (18 slots)

This is much larger than drift. `brand.css`'s `[data-brand='A'][data-theme='dark']`
block is **hand-authored literals with no reference to the brand primitives at all**
(unlike its light block, which aliases `var(--brandA-*)`). It describes a **warm/orange**
dark theme — hues 45 and 92 — while `brands/A/dark.json` describes a **teal** one —
hues 200, 183 and 80. They are not near-misses; they are different palettes.

Which one is "right" is a design decision and is NOT settled here. What is recorded is
that adopting the generated bridge changes brand A's dark appearance substantially,
and that this must be an explicit call in s168 rather than a side effect.

The 20 `--theme-status-*` slots agree exactly; every non-status slot disagrees.

## Slots the bridge does not emit

`brand.css` assigns 41 `--theme-*` slots; the bridge emits 38. The three below have no
brand token behind them, so generating them would mean the build inventing design
values. Under a bridge block they keep their `:root` value, which is the brand-agnostic
neutral reference palette from `src/tokens/themes/theme0/focus.json`.

| slot | why not bridged |
|---|---|
| `--theme-focus-ring-outer` | no brand token; brand.css derives it via a hand-authored color-mix() ratio that varies per theme |
| `--theme-focus-ring-inner` | no brand token; brand.css aliases surface.canvas in light but hardcodes a literal in dark |
| `--theme-focus-text` | no brand token; brand.css aliases text.accent in light but hardcodes a literal in dark and HighlightText in hc |
