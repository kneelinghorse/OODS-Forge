## 1. What this sprint is for, in one sentence

The objects Derek's own products are actually made of — CMOS's decisions, sprints and sessions, Hive's people and clusters — are born into Forge's registry from their live records, each arriving with the screen that needed it certified usable; and when a composition is previewed, the decisions and evidence about that object appear beside it.

Roadmap exit (§8): each slice is proven on a real screen, and the registry grows because a real screen needed it, never speculatively.

## 3. Settled decisions (build from these; do not reopen)

1. **Objects are born from the live record, never from a specification.** Every shape comes from a direct read of the store it belongs to, with the row counts recorded. **No object lands without the screen that needed it**, composed and opened as a running app in both frameworks in the same mission. A registry row with no screen is not a deliverable this sprint accepts.
2. **Reuse is proven before anything is authored.** Each of m02 and m03 produces a fit read first: every field mapped to a reused object, a reused trait, a new trait, or "not modelled" with a reason. CMOS's mission is tested against `research/Mission` and Hive's person against `User`/`Relationship` before a new object is written. This is the anti-speculation gate, and it is a receipt, not a claim.
3. **Supersession is the genuinely new trait.** A decision replaced by a later decision (`superseded_by` plus `status` active | superseded | archived | stale) is a self-referencing lineage no existing trait expresses — and it is the same shape Forge already uses for composition versions and for `accepted.json`'s supersession. `lifecycle/Supersedable` is authored once and used by both, or the sprint types the measured reason one trait cannot serve both.
4. **Hive's article question is settled by measurement, not preference.** The two field sets are compared and the receipt states whether `content/Article` carries both or a distinct object was authored. Neither is forced into the other to keep the object count down.
5. **Context enters through the caller; Forge reaches into nothing.** `design.preview` gains a `context` input carrying items the agent already fetched with the tools that exist (`cmos_context` search, `cmos_decisions` search, `tracelab_search`, the Hive tools). Forge validates them, keys them to the composition's object name and Forge URN, stores them on the version record beside `measurements`, and renders them. Forge opens no database, store or network connection belonging to another product: a direct reader would couple the bundle to CMOS 3.0.0's schema and break it for everyone who is not Derek, and the bundle must keep passing its E2E with 0 typed limits. This is the first cross-tool join and it needs no binding table, exactly as §8 says.
6. **Context is provenanced and durable, or it is not shown.** Every item names its source, the query that produced it and when it was fetched; items older than the composition's head are marked; an object with no results shows what was searched and that nothing was found. No placeholder, no invented item, no silent staleness. An item naming an object the composition does not contain is refused with a typed error and nothing is written.
7. **One renderer, two surfaces.** `shell.ts` gains `renderContext`, used by the browser page and by the conversation app through the Sprint 202 callback pattern; the page's output stays byte-identical for a version that carries no context.
8. **The carries close first (m01)**, at the producer, receipted before and after: axe-core inside the conversation view or the descope re-typed with a measured reason and an honest panel; `heading-order` to zero; the adapter's description check to 16/16 with the receipt saying whether the descriptions or the verb list moved; Derek's host receipts recorded if those runs happened, never staged.
