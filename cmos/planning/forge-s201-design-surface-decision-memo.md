## 3. Settled decisions (build from these; do not reopen)

1. **The preview is the generated app running, served by the bridge, and it ships in the bundle.** A preview host inside `packages/mcp-bridge` (`/preview/...`) compiles the generated artifact at request time with esbuild (TSX) and `@vue/compiler-sfc` (SFC) into one ESM module per composition version, and serves it with prebuilt host runtimes built once at package build time: React, ReactDOM, Vue, the foundation packages' `dist`, `@oods/component-styles` CSS and the token CSS. No Vite, no npm install, no Playwright is needed to view. The page mounts the artifact exactly as `scripts/design-loop/serve.ts` `consumerEntries` does (deterministic field model, action events, `data-brand`/`data-theme` on the document). The bundle's production closure gains esbuild (with its darwin-arm64, darwin-x64, linux-x64 and linux-arm64 binaries), `@vue/compiler-sfc`, react, react-dom and vue; `THIRD-PARTY-NOTICES.md` and the SBOM regenerate; the platform matrix is stated in `install.md`. The stdio adapter starts the preview host lazily on 127.0.0.1 when a tool needs it and returns the URL, so Claude Desktop and Cursor users get it with no bridge of their own. `design.preview` works from the extracted archive: `OODS-N019` closes, `portable:e2e` calls it, the ledger reads 19 / 19 / 0.
2. **Compositions become durable, with versions and lineage.** A composition store under the schema store root (`.oods/compositions/<id>/versions/<n>.json`): the compose inputs, the schema, the parent version, the operation that produced it, the Forge head (`build-revision.json`), the artifact hashes per framework, and the measurements. `design.compose` returns `compositionId` and `version` beside the existing `schemaRef` (which keeps its TTL for the transient path). One URL per version: `/preview/<compositionId>/<version>?framework=react|vue&brand=A|B&theme=light|dark|hc`. Every page shows its lineage: composition, version, parent, operation, head.
3. **Brand and theme are switches on the page**, re-mounting with `data-brand`/`data-theme` and the matching token CSS; width is a control (390 / 820 / 1440 and free). Both frameworks always.
4. **Side by side with what-changed.** `/compare/<id>@<v>/<id>@<v>` (or two compositions) shows both running apps and a what-changed panel: regions added, removed or reordered; slot component changes; prop changes; field order; the seed; artifact files whose hash moved; and both measurement panels. The receipt comparison in `scripts/design-loop/diff.ts` is reused for the fields that apply.
5. **Measurement beside the render, nothing claimed that was not run.** The `code.generate` `validationReceipt` (checks run and `notChecked`), `artifact.certify` for every placed chart, and axe-core run inside the running page for the current brand and theme, posted back to the host and stored on the version. The panel names what was measured and what was not.
6. **Editing writes back into the composition.** Four operations in the page and through the tool: reorder a region, swap a slot's component for one of the composer's own candidates, reorder the fields of a region, change the sample-data seed. Each operation is recorded on a new version with its parent; the server re-composes through the override surface (extended with region order, field order and seed) and re-generates; the schema is never hand-edited; the new version opens with its lineage. `design.preview` gains an `action` (`render` default, `compare`, `edit`, `versions`) so an agent can drive the same operations; the advertised contract change regenerates the registry, ledger and docs.
7. **The craft list is closed in this sprint, by the agent, at the producer.** Every `#2046` item, the Relationship graph title and labels, the sankey title overlap, and the Sprint 200 residue (decision 1 takes the preview items; the root Storybook chrome and the payload-to-file option are here). Proof is the design-loop receipts and screenshots of the affected checkpoints in both frameworks at 390/820/1440; runtime hashes move once, attributed in the ledger.
8. **Program rules.** No consumer notices (`#1979`). `builderSelfCertified:false`. Generated files only through their generators (`--check` after). Never build in the primary checkout. No hosted CI. Every pin moves at most once, in `artifacts/product-reality/sprint-201/golden-ledger.json`; sealed `sprint-195..200` receipts stay byte-identical; the viz registries and certified matrix do not move. No invented legal, commercial or brand content (`#2088`). The review closes the sprint with carries and never adds missions. Nothing in this sprint is a demo, a proof for Derek to perform, or a stub: a mission is complete when the capability works from the served bridge and from the extracted bundle and its tests fail without it.
